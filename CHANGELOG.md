# Changelog

All notable changes to AgeForge are documented here.

---

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **The tech tree is a map.** `research` opens the tree itself instead of a list: lanes across, ages down, a badge and a name for each tech, and lines from what a tech needs. The arrows move from tech to tech and the view follows, `Tab` steps through the techs you can start, `PgUp` and `PgDn` zoom between big badges and a one-line-a-tech overview that fits every lane, and `Home` returns to your age. The Help panel lists the keys, and the lane row counts the lanes off screen at either end (`2▸`) and names the one off to the left on a wide terminal (`◂ FAITH`). Every state has a shape of its own (a tick for researched, a bar filling along the bottom while it runs, a dashed frame while it waits, a shaded block for the next age, a star on a keystone, `▸1` for its place in your plan), so the tree reads in every theme, and `map glyphs ascii` draws it in plain ASCII. Later ages stay a count. See [Reading the Tech Tree](site/docs/technologies.md#reading-the-tech-tree).
- **A card for every tech.** `Enter` on a tech opens its card: what it does in plain sentences, what it opens, its price and its time at your research speed, what it builds on and how many later techs build on it. A second `Enter` does what the card's last line says: starts the tech, or adds it to the build plan when the slot is busy, the knowledge is short or the tech still waits. Nothing spends knowledge on the first `Enter`. `research card <tech>` opens a card from the prompt, and `research tree close` and `research tree far` set the zoom.
- **Panels say when a command waits for a tech.** The Army, Trade and Factions panels and `campaign list` carry the refusal's own words (`Campaigns need Military Tactics first. Research it to send one.`) once the tech's age is reached.
- Tests: the tree is rendered at 80x24, 100x30, 120x40 and 144x46, at both zooms, in every theme and in the plain glyph tier. They fail on a badge that is not whole, an emblem off its centre cell, a name plate that is cut, and a connector that leads nowhere or runs under a tech.
- Smoke: an advance that waits because a fated doom has to settle first is counted as `fate_at_advance`, no longer as a bot error.
- **Tech bonuses always count.** A tech's bonus now sits in a layer of its own, applied after the x3 caps, where Era Mastery is: `buildings and workers × pools (clamped at ×3) × (1 + tech bonuses)`. Techs used to add into the same pools as milestones and wonders, and from about the Electric Age most of what they promised was swallowed by the clamp (the all-production techs alone promised +950% and delivered a fraction). Inside the layer, bonuses on the same thing add up (two +5% give +10%) and nothing caps the sum. A tech that cuts a price or a time multiplies with the others of its kind and has a floor: building costs 10% of the price, construction 40% of its time, research 50%. The `rates` breakdown carries the layer on its Research line, the Research panel lists what all your techs come to together, and no tech is ever tagged "capped". See [How Tech Bonuses Stack](site/docs/technologies.md#how-tech-bonuses-stack).
- **Commands that open with a tech.** Campaigns wait for Military Tactics, the Naval Expedition for Navigation, the black market for Mercantilism and the Rail Freight route for Railroads. Used before its tech, a command is refused and names it (`Campaigns need Military Tactics first. Research it to send one.`), and the tech's effect list says what it opens. Five more locks are in the table and wait for techs that arrive with later updates (trade routes, the other scouting expeditions, diplomacy, festivals, the Warp Commerce route): until then those commands are open.
- **A game saved before the command locks keeps what it was using.** In the age the save is in every command stays open, and a command it was already using (a campaign under way, a route running, a smuggling run made) stays open for the rest of that run, whatever it has researched. One log line says so on the first load. A new run meets the locks.
- **New kinds of tech effect.** A tech can now raise every store or housing by a percentage, cut construction time, cut research time, and move one number of a mechanic: the market's fee, a trade route's time, a scouting expedition's time, what a raid takes, how long a set of faction deals lasts, what a gift costs, a festival's price and its cooldown, and what a hand gather brings.
- Tests: **the headroom test** (`go test ./config -run TestTechHeadroom`) takes every tech and fails if any sum has reached a floor or a cap. The bonus truth guard now measures the tech layer in every age and reads every other bonus per point of it; its allow-list lost the gold and knowledge caps, which no longer bite. Every tech's promises are available as data (`config.TechChecks`: `rate(food) x1.10`, `market_fee -0.03`, `allowed(campaigns) 0>1`).
- Smoke: the static report gains **Feature locks** (which are live and which wait for their tech) and **Bonus pools against their caps** (how close each pool gets to x3, age by age, for a player holding every milestone, wonder and monument). The progression report records each age's knowledge per hour and how full the all-production pool was when the age ended.
- **Keystone techs.** From the Stone Age on, each age's wonder needs one tech of its own age before it can be built: its keystone (Stoneworking for the Great Monolith, Mathematics for the Colosseum, Philosophy for the Parthenon, Theology for the Great Library, and so on to Transcendence for the Singularity Core; 20 in all). The Sacred Grove needs none, so nothing stands between a new game and the Stone Age, and Stonehenge has none until the tech it will stand on exists. The wonder's bank does not wait: deposits and overflow fill it while you research, and only `build <wonder>` is refused, naming the tech (`Colosseum needs Mathematics first. Research it to build here.`). Since the next age needs the wonder, the keystone and the techs it stands on are the only research a run must do: 43 of the 77 techs. The Wonders panel, `wonder`, the age bar and the age splash show the keystone and whether it is researched, the Research panel and `research list` mark every keystone with a ★ and the wonder it opens, and the log says so when a finished keystone opens its wonder. A wonder in the build plan waits for its keystone and starts by itself.
- **A game saved before keystones gets an age of grace.** The age the save is in is exempt: its wonder builds without the tech, and the lock starts when you next advance. One log line says so on the first load. Everything researched stays researched and every wonder built or under construction stays. Saves carry two new fields, `tree_version` and `tree_grace_age`; older saves still pass their signature check.
- **A Succumb earns Era Mastery.** Succumbing ends the run the way a prestige does for mastery: every age the fallen run completed gains a level, so the rebuild starts on known ground (a first run that falls in the Renaissance Age comes back with the Primitive to the Medieval Age at 2x, and its first ages at the 4x catch-up). It still earns no prestige points. No age is counted twice for one run: the prestige after a Succumb raises only what the rebuilt run completed. Before, a Succumb raised nothing and the fallen run's ages were credited at the next prestige instead. The catastrophe box lists the mastery among what a Succumb gives, and the new run's log says which ages gained.
- **A wonder in your build plan takes overflow.** "Overflow pays the plan" skipped wonders, so with wonder overflow off a planned wonder's bank only filled when you deposited by hand. A wonder queued in the plan now takes what full stores would waste, in plan order, into its own bank, up to what the bank still lacks, while you play and while you are away; the welcome-back summary counts it. With wonder overflow on (the default) nothing changes: the age's wonder already has first call on overflow.
- **An early taste says what it is.** Prestige opens at the Medieval Age and pays little that early. `prestige confirm` before the Modern Age, and the first lines of the new run, now say so plainly: "This is an early taste: a prestige from the Medieval Age pays 9 prestige points, for the 5 ages this run completed. Going deeper pays far more: each era's ages are worth 3 times the era before, and a full run, 7 ages further on, pays 120 prestige points." Once you have seen the Modern Age the line names it ("and a run to the Modern Age pays 120 prestige points").
- **The game says when a cap holds a bonus back.** All production counts up to +200% (x3), each resource's own production the same, and research speed up to +100%. Until now a bonus past a cap did nothing and nothing said so. The Stats panel's Active Multipliers now shows what counts, with the earned total beside it (`All production +200% capped at +200%: +405% earned`). The Research, Milestones and Wonders panels put a note beside every bonus a cap holds back: a tech or reward you already hold shows where its pool stands, and one you can still earn says what the cap would leave of it (`capped: no effect now`, `capped: +5% of it counts now`) before you spend anything. The `festival` command warns before you pay, and the log adds a line when a bonus you just earned is capped: a tech, a milestone, a wonder, an awakening, an epoch event, a festival or a boon. The caps themselves are unchanged.
- Tests: **the bonus truth guard** (`go test ./game -run TestBonusTruth`). Every bonus the game promises is switched on in a real engine and the thing its text names is measured: over 800 promises from every tech, milestone, chain, building, wonder, monument, event, awakening, epoch event, boon, allied bonus, Succumb legacy, the Cosmic Legacy and the festival, plus Era Mastery, morale, ruins and the legacy kit. Each is read in a clean engine, where it must deliver exactly what it says, and in the pacing model's typical player in the age it is earned and later ones, where a cap may hold it back. The promises come from config, so a new tech or milestone is measured without touching the test, and an effect type or target the guard has no meter for fails it. The promises the game does not keep are in a four-line allow-list, each with its reason (the caps, pending their redesign); a bonus a cap holds back must carry the game's "capped" note, and no other bonus may. `BONUS_TRUTH_REPORT=<file>` writes the full table.
- **The legacy kit**: the prestige shop now sells three items, each bought once, that carry a run's automation across every prestige and Succumb. **Plan Template** (`legacy_plan`, 9 points): the game records your build plan as you write it, each item tagged with the age you added it in (deals are left out), and at the start of every run and every advance it adds that age's part back to the plan, the advance item included, so a plan written once chains ages while you are away. One log line says what went in ("Plan Template: added 12 items for the Bronze Age."). The techs you planned with `plan research` come along with the rest of the plan, in the age you planned them in; the plan is the only research queue, and the game never chooses what you research next. **Worker Shares** (`legacy_workers`, 36): your worker shares carry over into each new run instead of going back to auto. **Old Friends** (`legacy_factions`, 54): every civilization you have met is met again as soon as your age reaches its own, with no expedition and no fallback wait, at neutral opinion. The whole kit costs 99 points: a Medieval Age taste buys the template, and a first Modern Age run buys the rest and leaves 21. The game remembers your plan, worker shares and civilizations from your first prestige or Succumb whether or not you own the items, so an item bought later works on the run you are in at once. `prestige shop` lists the kit with each price or "owned" and what the kit remembers; `prestige` and the Stats panel show the items you own.
- Smoke: the bot prestiges at the Modern Age by default, and a new `taste` style prestiges at the Medieval Age. The progression report checks that depth pays: the points per day of a first run that prestiges at the Medieval Age, the Modern Age and the Cyberpunk Age (a run through the Digital Age) must each beat the one before by 1.25x. The static check covers the depth weights, the wiki's points table and a second taste's share (under 10%). Every prestige is checked for the kit's carry-over (worker shares, the template's part at each advance, old friends met at their age). The `veteran` scenario, in every tier, buys the kit after a scripted prestige, checks each item (and that a tech the player planned comes along with the template while nothing researches by itself), and checks the refund of a signed level-5 save from the old shop. The idle scenario's veteran check-ins now carry the kit, with the canned memory of a veteran's last run in `smoke/testdata/veteran_kit.json`, and their watch lines became limits enforced under `-pacing enforce`: 1.6x at 3 hours and 2.6x at 8 hours of the active veteran's time to the first prestige (measured 1.46x and 2.40x; without the kit they were 1.88x and 3.41x, and with no techs in the template 1.65x and 3.08x). New `-kit` and `-dump-legacy <file>` flags in `cmd/smoke`: `-kit` gives every bot run the veteran's kit, and `-dump-legacy` writes the bot's builds (an upgrade as a build of the new tier), the techs it researched (as planned research items), its trades and its advances into the plan log as if planned and saves the kit's memory after the first prestige, which is how the canned kit is made.
- **Era Mastery**: ages you have completed run faster on later runs. Each age's mastery is the number of prestige runs that completed it, 10 at most; a prestige raises every age below the run's furthest age by one level, and mastery stays fixed during a run. An age at mastery m runs k = 1 + √m times as fast (2x at mastery 1, 2.4x at 2, 4.2x at 10): production and storage are multiplied by k (after the ×3 cap and the food drain, so a store holds the same hours of income), build and research times are divided by k, and so are a fated doom's window and the harbinger's lead, so both fall inside the shorter era. Events, raids, trade routes, expeditions and other timers keep their length. The frontier (mastery 0) runs at 1x, so a first run is unchanged. Ages 6 or more behind your record (the deepest age you have ever entered) run at least 4x, after a Succumb too. When the speed drops, stock above the shrunk cap is kept until spent (the grace rule). A Succumb raises no mastery. Shown in `prestige`, the Stats panel, a new Era Mastery section in the Epoch panel, the `rates` breakdown and a log line on entering each age; research and wonder build times shown are the real ones. Measured with the smoke bot on the one-week curve: a second run reaches the Modern Age in 2.3 days (the first takes 5.3) and ends an age deeper, and a veteran gets there in 1.3.
- Dev console: `/mastery <age|all> <0-10>` sets Era Mastery for one age or every age, and `/record <age_key>` sets the record, for testing.
- Smoke: a new `veteran` scenario plays the veteran preset (mastery 10 through the Space Age, record in the Interstellar Age): per PR to the Iron Age on one seed, nightly to a Modern Age prestige on three seeds, with each age from the Bronze Age within 0.5x to 2x of its target ÷ k, the Primitive and Stone Ages under an hour together and the Modern Age in 1.1 to 1.5 days. New `-preset veteran|returning` and `-push-cycles` flags in `cmd/smoke`. The full tier's progression now plays two cycles, the second a push (it plays as long as the first took, then prestiges), which must cover the first run's ages at least 1.9x faster and end at least one age deeper; the old run on to the Digital Age is gone. The idle scenario also reports how long veteran check-in players at 3 and 8 hours take against an active veteran (watch lines 2.2x and 4.1x, warn only), and the determinism check replays one veteran-preset seed.
- **Mid-age unlocks**: four new techs, one each in the Atomic, Information, Cyberpunk and Fusion Ages, open a building partway through the age. Those ages used to give an active player most of a day with no new building or tech while they saved up for the wonder. **Civilian Reactors** (Atomic) opens the Nuclear Plant, **Internet of Things** (Information) the Smart Farm and the Smart Complex, **Holography** (Cyberpunk) the Holographic Theater and **Maglev Transit** (Fusion) the Energy Exchange. Each comes after its age's other techs and is priced in knowledge so it lands around the middle of the age. Until its tech is done a gated building is missing from the build list, and `build` and `upgrade` name the tech it needs. The build plan takes it early and holds it until the tech is done. A save that already has copies keeps them. The tech tree now has 77 techs, and the "research every tech" milestone asks for all 77. Smoke: an age whose longest stretch with no new building type or tech runs over 12 hours for the active bot (median across seeds, first cycle) now fails under `-pacing enforce` for every age up to the Fusion Age (the nightly grades the ages to the Modern Age, the weekly the rest). The Space Age on still go one to three days with nothing new and are reported only. The Information Age is held to 1.2x its target, and the static gate check fails a gate that needs a building behind a tech its age can't finish. See Mid-age unlocks on the Technologies wiki page.
- **Worker shares**: workers now arrive and go to work on their own. A share is a domain's percent of your whole workforce, idle workers included (`workers share knowledge 40`; 0 to 100, decimals and a % sign allowed). Domains without one are on auto and split what the set shares leave in proportion to their worker slots; every domain starts on auto, so workers follow your buildings. Set shares that add up to more than 100% are each scaled down to fit (the auto domains then get only what overflows from full domains), a share of 0 keeps a domain empty, `workers share <domain> auto` and `workers share auto` put one or every domain back on auto, and bare `workers share` lists each domain's share, workers and slots (`Knowledge: 40% (set), 8 workers in 10 slots`). A routine keeps to the shares every 10 seconds of play and once per step of the offline catch-up: it moves workers out of domains set to 0, puts idle workers to work in the domain furthest below its share, and, with auto-recruit on (the default; `workers auto-recruit off` turns it off), recruits into empty worker slots as far as housing allows while the net food rate stays at or above a margin (one worker's food, or 5% of the food production if that is more). It never recruits more workers than there are empty slots. A recruit the food can't feed still comes as a food worker when a food building has a free slot, since that worker grows more food than they eat, so a small food share never stops growth; nothing is recruited while workers are starving. The routine never takes a worker out of a building except out of a domain set to 0, so your own `assign` and `unassign` stick, and it waits a minute after any worker command (`recruit`, `assign`, `unassign`, `dismiss`, `sell`, `upgrade`) before it places anyone. A share command moves workers once to match. The Workers panel gains a Shares section (what auto-recruit is doing, and each domain's share, workers and slots), what the routine does is a routine line in the log, and the welcome back says what it did while you were away. Shares, the auto-recruit switch and the minute's wait are saved; prestige and Succumb put the shares back on auto and keep the auto-recruit switch, and a new game resets both. Smoke: the idle style leaves its workers to the shares on auto instead of recruiting and assigning by hand, and `-no-shares` makes it recruit and assign by hand with auto-recruit off, to measure what shares are worth. In the smoke suite's idle style (4 seeds), checking in every 8 hours now reaches the first prestige in about 5.9 days (was 6.5) and every 3 hours in about 3.8 (unchanged), with starvation deaths down from hundreds per run to a few dozen at most. See Worker Shares on the Workers & Domains wiki page.
- **The sky arc**: from the Space Age both map styles leave the ground, and each of the last five ages has a scene of its own with its own palette, landmark, traffic and ambient motion. It is still your civilization: every lineage becomes one kind of thing up there (homes are habitat modules in orbit, colony arks in deep space, decks on the starbase), its buildings are its modules, and the scene grows as you build without ever moving what is drawn. **Space Age**: actual space. The planet's curved limb fills the bottom of the map, its night side your own land and sea with drifting clouds, amber city lights where the town stands and the civs you have met in their colors; the space elevator's tether rises to the station; shipyard slips with hulls rising, solar wings, foundry tanks, relay satellites, an asteroid belt with mining outposts and drones, a base on the moon; climbers, shuttles and satellites. **Interstellar Age**: the home system shrinks to an orrery in the corner, violet and teal nebula wisps thread the dark, each lineage settles a colony world that grows with it and calls home with a beacon, generation ships cross trailing their engines, and a warp gate's ring rises segment by segment from your engineering, its field opening once the Warp Nexus stands. **Galactic Age**: a starbase hub (a gold core, habitat decks, a docking ring with running lights, six pylons with the fleet docked) before the galaxy's arms and full-color nebulae, a star system with a trade lane for every civ met, starships jumping to warp in a streak of light, and the rare visitor's saucers as ordinary traffic, each species in its own color. **Quantum Age**: the starbase nests inside itself three times over, every structure in superposition and iridescent, probability clouds with sliding interference fringes, and an echo of the old town round it. **Transcendent Age**: a mandala of light on indigo, exactly symmetric about its core, with one ring for every era your run passed through (Stone innermost), each a band of its era's own glyph in its era's color, dark space between them, and a wave of light rolling out from the core ring by ring every few seconds. The age's own buildings are a crown of eight solid gold petals round a star of gold rays, whose glow grows as you build; the inspect cursor names the buildings you raised in each era. On the skyline the city, the far civs and the traffic are gone, since you are no longer bound to corporeal form: the mandala fills the view alone, `Tab` goes from the core out ring by ring through every building type still standing, and the arrows step round a ring and from ring to ring. The legend, inspect cursor, `Tab`, flows overlay and mini map work in every scene; in the roguelike the region zoom stays the known world and `PgDn` gives a closer view with names. The rare visitor keeps its surprise visits through the Interstellar Age and turns into traffic in the Galactic. See The sky on the Map wiki page.
- **Traffic on the roguelike map**: every era brings its own movers, each from the age that introduces it and retiring as its era passes. Hunters head for the hills in the Stone Era; ox carts and riders take the streets and rowboats the river in the Iron Era; wagons and sailing ships arrive in the Steel Era, and from the Industrial Age a railway crosses the town with steam trains trailing smoke; trams, steamships and the first motor cars come in the Electric Era; cars, trucks, planes overhead, container ships and freight trains in the Digital Era, with satellites in the night sky; maglevs take over the railway and drones and hovercars fill the Neon Era, with shuttles lifting off from the Space Age; orbital habitats drift over the Cosmic Era. People keep their stroll and vehicles are quicker by type (carts a little, trains and maglevs most). How much moves follows the size and bustle of the town. Traffic draws only on streets, rails, water and open sky, never over a building or a name; the legend lists what is about and the inspect cursor says what each one is ("A steam train, hauling ore."). The railway runs along one of the town plan's street rows, so it never moves as the town grows. The two map styles read one roster of what moves in which age, so the skyline agrees on when each vehicle arrives. Keep an eye on the sky in the late game. See [The Map](site/docs/map.md#traffic).
- **The Earth arc on the Map, the Modern Age to the Fusion Age**: every late age now reads at a glance, in both map styles. The land round your town becomes the age's city while your own buildings still make the town. **Modern**: glass downtown, suburbs on green lawns, parks fenced into their blocks, highways (headlights at night). **Information**: office blocks over most of the land, server farms, dishes and fiber lines, half the green left as parks and rooftop gardens, screens flickering, the first smog. **Digital**: one walled reserve and nothing else green, concrete, data halls glowing, landfill under the smog, drones. **Cyberpunk**: the megacity: not a tree left, megablocks, megacorp towers with their initials in neon, arcologies, flickering neon and holo-ads, toxic canals, three to five elevated sky rails with sky trains on each, drone swarms, hovercars and crowds, acid rain, night on the map; the busiest map in the game. **Fusion**: reactors in their plasma rings, conduits, maglev skyways, launch towers, clean cooling water and the space elevator's tether rising off the top of the map with its climbers. The skyline matches: a freeway and street trees, smog bands and dishes, the reserve, a megacity skyline with holo-ads, rails and rain, then reactor glow and the tether (which the cursor can inspect). The green fades by a tested rule (all of it in the Modern Age, about half in the Information Age, the reserve's 15% in the Digital Age, none from the Cyberpunk Age), a disparity guard keeps neighboring ages from looking alike, and the city is laid from your save's world alone, so it never reshuffles as your town grows. New movers: the news helicopter, the sky train, crowds and the tether climber; drones now arrive in the Digital Age. A busy roguelike legend pairs short labels so the traffic still fits. See [The Map](site/docs/map.md#the-city-from-the-modern-age-to-the-fusion-age).
- **The army defends you**: your Defense Rating (soldiers × 2 × (1 + military power)) now blunts losses. It is measured against the raid threat of the age you are in, which doubles every age (1,280 in the Iron Age, 2,560 in the Classical Age, and so on), and blunts 45% × defense / (defense + threat) of a raid: nothing with no soldiers, 22.5% when your defense matches the threat, never more than 45%. An Iron Age army does next to nothing against a Quantum Age raid, so a garrison has to keep up with the age. It blunts raid events (Bandit Raid, Pirate Attack, Tribal Raid, Beast Stampede, Data Breach, Corporate Espionage: the resources they steal and the workers they drive off), war raids from civilizations at war with you, and an Endure's building and stock losses. For an Endure the Harbinger's Brace applies first, then the garrison, and together they cut at most 60% of the unbraced loss (at least 8% of buildings fall, at most 66% of stock is kept). With no soldiers every loss is exactly what it was. The Army panel shows the threat, how much of a raid your garrison would blunt, what twice the garrison would do, and what the army has saved this run; raid, war raid and Endure log lines say what the garrison kept; the catastrophe modal and the harbinger's Brace preview show Endure's numbers with your garrison counted. See Defense on the Military wiki page.
- Smoke: `-army=on` makes the bot keep a modest garrison (four of the age's newest military building, bought from spare stock), the `styles` scenario gains a report-only `army` style, and the progression report gains an Army table (buildings lost to Endure, stock kept, garrison share, raids blunted).
- Smoke: the progression report grades the first run to the Modern Age against 4.8 to 6.2 days (the median across seeds, enforced with the per-age bands) and gains a "longest quiet" column, each age's longest stretch with no new building type and no new tech (reported only). The idle scenario plays the greedy bot on the same seeds and reports each check-in interval as a ratio to it. The static scenario checks that every harbinger Appease and Brace price fits the storage of its era's first age. The nightly runs as four shards side by side (progression seeds 1-4, seeds 5-8, styles and idle, the rest) and a merge job pools them into one report: `-only` and `-skip` pick a shard's scenarios, and `-merge` now merges any set of reports.
- Tests: `TestMilestonesAreFeasible` (`go test ./smoke`) proves from config that every milestone, chain and title can be completed, by the end of the last age before a full run's prestige (the Atomic Age) or of the milestone's own age: building counts against the most storage buildable in the building's own age, population against housing, structures built against what a run can hold, and resources, techs, wonders, soldiers and knowledge workers. Failures name the milestone, the requirement and the math. It replaces a check that only compared counts with max counts; the `static` smoke scenario runs it too and reports each milestone's earliest age and tightest requirement. The structures, soldiers, wonders and knowledge-worker thresholds moved from the engine into the milestone definitions.
- **Faction trade deals**: every civilization you have met offers a small, rotating set of trades, derived from its specialty, personality and strength and your standing, with no per-civ tables. Each deal reads from your side (`Buy: give 876M coal → get 966K food`): Buy the civ's specialty with your construction resources, Sell it what you hold most of for its specialty, trade the same for +5 standing (Favor), or, from isolationists, buy the next age's goods at a steep price (Rare). Where the market trades the pair, a deal pays 5-25% better than the market, never better than parity. Mercantile civs offer more and better deals, aggressive ones fewer and worse, isolationists one or two rare ones; friendly and allied civs offer more, better and bigger. Civs at war, under embargo, rival or at opinion -50 or lower offer nothing, and standing from deals stops at opinion 50. Offers rotate after an hour of play and on an age advance, and the timer doesn't run offline. The Factions panel shows each civ's deals; `diplomacy deals [civ]` lists them, `diplomacy accept <civ> <n>` takes one, and `plan deal <civ> <n>` queues one in the build plan. Deals are saved with your game. The smoke bot ignores them by default; `-deals=on` measures them. See Trade deals on the Trade & Diplomacy wiki page.
- **Build plan** (`plan`): queue builds, techs, trades and an advance, and the game starts each one, in order, as the resources come in, while you play and while you are away. Items are paid for when they start, not when you add them. A waiting item holds back its next price from the items below it, so the order is your priority, but items below still start with what it doesn't need. Techs start one at a time in plan order (a research queue). `plan trade <from> <to> [amount]` sells as the resource comes in once the market has recovered from the last sale; `plan advance` advances as soon as the age is ready, and the next age's buildings and techs can be planned to wait for it. Items that can never start drop out with a log line, copies the plan builds are staffed from idle workers, and the plan is saved and cleared by prestige, Succumb and a new game. The Plan panel shows each item's price and status, with keys to reorder and remove. See the new Build Plan wiki page.
- **Offline catch-up runs in one-minute steps**: the plan starts items as the day's income pays for them, the caps apply along the way, and construction and research finish while you are away (they used to wait for you to come back). A day away takes milliseconds; with nothing planned or under construction it pays what it always did.

And a **rotating pool of log flavor** fires on notable moments — a building finishing, an age turning over, a research breakthrough, the onset and end of famine, surviving a catastrophe — drawn at random per event so the same moment reads a little differently each time, and rate-limited on high-volume moments so the tick log never turns to wallpaper. Expedition results, faction standoffs, encounters turned away by a full court, and war raids go further: they draw from a **procedural flavor generator** instead of fixed three-line banks — about 2,100 hand-written sentences, split by era so a Stone Age raid and a Quantum Age raid read like different places (from the Information Age on, two lines in three are written for your era, and the kitchen, the back stairs and the bread stay in the ages that have them), fed through one stream per log that never repeats a sentence inside a 32-line window. ### Balance
- **Research is priced by the age.** No tech has a hand-typed cost or time any more. A tech's research time is a share of its age's research cap (an eighth of the age's target): 40% for a spine tech (one a keystone stands on), 50% for a keystone or an optional tech. Its cost is a share of the age's knowledge budget, which is 90% of what a well-run civilization makes of knowledge in the age's target time (50% in the Primitive Age, 30% in the Transcendent Age): a spine tech weighs 0.6, a keystone 0.8, an optional tech 1.0. So what a run must research is the quick, cheap part, research lasts the whole age, and when techs are added to an age each one there gets cheaper while the age's total stays put. The early techs come within reach of their own ages: the Stone Age's four cost 3.8K knowledge together, down from 28.5K (the age makes about 4K), and Tool Making costs 61, down from 800. From the Classical Age on research becomes a real part of each age instead of an afterthought: the Classical Age's three techs cost 1.2M (were 60K), the Renaissance's four 80M (were 190K), the Atomic Age's four 5.7B. Research times roughly halve. This replaces the hand-set prices of the mid-age unlocks and of the Cyberpunk and Fusion Age techs listed below: those ages' budgets come out close to what they were (Cyberpunk 18.9B against 20.3B, Fusion 22.9B against 19.7B), and the mid-age techs come late because of what they stand on. The wiki's tech tables list every cost and time.
- **The Renaissance's producers repay more slowly.** Their payback goes from 1.3x to 1.7x what the curve gives (about 6.5 hours): the University makes 61.1 knowledge a tick (was 79.9), the Exchange 1.11K gold (was 1.45K), the Mill 153 steel (was 200), the Foundry 209 steel (was 273) and the Coal Mine 85 coal (was 111). The age was paced by two things, the slower payback and a 30M knowledge requirement, and the requirement is gone. Patronage's price stands in for it on a first run, but on known ground the market buys the knowledge as fast as everything else moves, and the age ran at half its length there.
- **No age asks for knowledge.** The advances into the Stone to Industrial Ages each asked for a knowledge amount (150 up to 30M), which had you saving the same knowledge your research wanted. Those eight requirements are gone: an age's knowledge goes to research, and the keystone is what the advance needs of it. A harbinger's Brace, priced off what the era's advances ask for, loses its knowledge part with them (Iron Era: 26.4K stone, 6.36K iron, 21.6K gold; Steel Era: 1.8M gold, 288K steel).
- **The Cosmic Legacy works in every age.** Its +10% all production sat in the all-production pool, which the x3 cap fills by about the Victorian Age, so from there on it did nothing. It now multiplies everything a resource makes by 1.1 after the cap and every other bonus, before the food your workers eat is taken off (so it never deepens a food shortage). The `rates` breakdown has a Cosmic Legacy part, Active Multipliers shows it beside the pool (`Cosmic Legacy ×1.10`), and it no longer carries a "capped" note.
- **Appease is priced on the warning, not on the era.** A doom's harbinger gives you 20% to 60% of one age, but Appease cost a quarter of what the whole era makes in faith (and culture), so it could only be paid from faith saved before anyone warned you: the smoke bot could afford level 1 in 15 of 46 warnings (24 seeds to a Quantum Age prestige), against Brace in 41. Level 1 now costs 15% of what the age the harbinger arrives in makes at a moderate faith economy, three quarters of what the shortest warning brings in, and the price is fixed for the thread when the harbinger arrives. Level 2 still costs double, so both levels take 45% of the age's faith: a long warning, or faith kept in storage. Prices fall to between a twentieth and about two fifths of what they were (an Iron Age warning: 1.4K faith, was 14K; a Space Age one: 130M faith and 1.9B culture, was 340M and 5.2B). On the same seeds the bot now affords level 1 in 32 of 41 warnings, level 2 in 21 and Brace in 36. The Last Passage's thread has a price of its own (next entry). The `static` smoke check holds every price to the storage of the age the harbinger can arrive in.
- **The Last Passage can be appeased.** Its harbinger's Appease kept an era price: a quarter of what the Interstellar, Galactic and Quantum Ages make together, 3.1B faith and 48B culture, which is 1.8 times the culture the whole Interstellar Age makes. The smoke bot could pay it in 2 of 24 runs to a Quantum Age prestige. It is now priced the way every other thread is, on the age its harbinger arrives in: three quarters of what a moderate faith and culture economy makes in the thread's shortest warning. A doom's shortest warning is a fifth of the age. The Last Passage's thread comes as you enter the Cosmic Era and lasts until you prestige, so its warning is longer: two thirds of the Interstellar Age. Level 1 costs 890M faith and 14B culture: half of what the Interstellar Age makes, a little over three times a doom foretold in the same age, and about 32 hours of what five copies of every faith and culture building bring in. Level 2 costs the same again (see "Second levels are worth buying" below), which takes playing the age out or a stock kept from before. A thread already running takes the new price, and a level already bought stays bought. The new price has not been measured with the smoke bot yet. The `static` smoke check now holds every level-1 Appease to its warning (a moderate economy must make it inside the shortest one) and the Last Passage's above a doom's foretold in the same age.
- **Bracing for the Last Passage takes effort, and appeasing it is worth it.** Brace on the Last Passage's thread cost what a doom's does, 12% of the largest requirement of the era's advances: 1.56T dark matter and 75.6B titanium, which a moderate Interstellar economy makes in under 7 ticks. For that, an Endure kept 70% of the run's prestige points in place of 50% (85% at level 2, for double). The smoke bot bought it on arrival in 24 runs of 24, and next to it Appease was poor value. Brace is now priced on the thread's warning, as its Appease is: level 1 costs half of what a moderate economy makes of the same two resources in that warning, a third of what the Interstellar Age makes, which is 8.9Q dark matter and 11Q titanium and about 21 hours of income. The two answers are set against each other: with nothing bought each first level saves the same expected share of the run (3% at mid faith), Appease level 1 is the dearer and saves about two thirds of what Brace level 1 does per hour of income. To get there the Last Passage's warning went from the whole Interstellar Age to two thirds of it, which is what brought Appease level 1 down from 1.4B faith and 21B culture (the price in the entry above is the new one). An ordinary doom's Brace is unchanged (the Reality Tear's has its own entry below). A thread already running takes the new prices, and a level already bought stays bought. Neither price has been measured with the smoke bot. The `static` smoke check holds the Last Passage's level-1 Brace to at least ten hours of a moderate economy's income, made inside the warning, and `TestLastPassageChoice` pins the value ratio. The wiki has the table: [Brace or Appease against the Last Passage](site/docs/harbinger.md#brace-or-appease-against-the-last-passage).
- **Faith strength: the faith bands can be reached, by a town that works at it.** The faith bands (a doom strikes 90%, 75% or 60% of the time, the Last Passage comes 18%, 15% or 12%, an epoch brings a good event 40%, 50% or 60%) read your faith as a share of its storage. But faith has no store of its own: it sits in the general store, which is sized for building materials and grows about tenfold an age while faith income doubles. Against the least storage the age gates force on anyone, an economy three times as devoted as the moderate one, saving through a doom's longest warning, reached 20% in the Iron Age, 21% in the Classical Age and under 5% from the Medieval Age on. So in every era every roll read the bottom band, and nothing you did with faith changed the odds. Faith strength is now your devotion times the share of your faith you have kept, divided by four and a half. Devotion is what your own faith buildings have made this run against a moderate set (five fully staffed copies of every faith building your age has), measured in your own town, so its bonuses, morale, Era Mastery and time away cancel out. **A typical town stays where it was:** a moderate town that never spends its faith reads 22%, the bottom band, in every age, so the odds the game was balanced and measured at are still what most players get. The middle band takes about 1.13 times the moderate set (twice the set reads 44%), the top band about 3.4 times, and spending faith lowers the strength by the share spent. Faith from wonders, techs and events is yours to spend but does not count: Stonehenge alone makes over eight times what a moderate Bronze Age set does, and no building the game requires of you can raise your band. Nothing changes at an advance, faith is only read, never clamped or taken, and the measure is three running totals saved with your game (a save from before it is read as a moderate town). The faith row, the `catastrophe` command and the Harbinger panel show the strength the rolls read ("faith strength 22% (devotion 1.0x, 100% of your faith kept)"), and the faith row no longer promises a prestige bonus that nothing gave. The `static` smoke check walks towns with none, one, two, three and three and a half moderate sets through the whole run with their hoards carried along, and fails if a moderate town leaves the bottom band or a devoted one cannot reach its own. See [Faith](site/docs/faith.md#faith-threshold-bands).
- **The Reality Tear is worth bracing for.** Its Brace kept the Cosmic Era's own price, 1.56T dark matter and 75.6B titanium, under 7 ticks of what a moderate Interstellar economy makes. It is now priced on its warning, as the Last Passage's is: level 1 costs five sixths of what a moderate economy makes in a doom's shortest warning, a sixth of the age its harbinger arrives in and half the Last Passage's price. That is 4.5Q dark matter and 5.5Q titanium in the Interstellar Age (93Q and 5.5Q from the Galactic Age on), about ten and a half hours of income. Every other era's Brace is unchanged. A thread already running takes the new price, and a level already bought stays bought. The `static` smoke check's ten-hour floor now covers both Cosmic Era threads.
- **Second levels are worth buying in the Cosmic Era.** Level 2 of Brace and of Appease cost double level 1 and did less, so per hour of income it saved 0.375 of what level 1 does (the Last Passage's Brace) or 0.3 (either thread's Appease). On the Last Passage's thread and the Reality Tear's, level 2 now costs the same again as level 1: 8.9Q dark matter and 11Q titanium, or 890M faith and 14B culture, for the Last Passage; 4.5Q and 5.5Q, or 270M and 4.1B, for the Reality Tear. It saves 0.75 and 0.6 of what level 1 does per hour, and the Reality Tear's Brace level 2 softens an Endure by as much as level 1 for the same price. `TestSecondLevelsWorthBuying` holds each to at least a half. No effect changed. Every other era's level 2 still costs double: there an Appease level 2 saves 0.3 of what level 1 does per hour and a Brace level 2 a half.
- **Ancient Knowledge never floors research.** Succumb's research bonus was +25% research speed per epoch, taken off a tech's listed time: four epochs brought every tech to a single tick and the fifth and sixth added nothing. It is now research time ×0.8 for each epoch you have succumbed in (×0.64 at two, ×0.26 at all six), applied after research speed and before Era Mastery. One epoch gives a little less than before (×0.8 against ×0.75) and every epoch now counts. It has its own line in the Research panel, the Stats panel and the catastrophe box, and is no longer part of the Research speed pool.
- **Worker output bonuses work.** Tool Making (+15%), Bronze Working (+10%), Road Building (+10%), Neural Interface (+30%) and the Jacked In milestone (+15%) promised more worker output and did nothing: the bonus was applied to a worker rate that has been empty since worker output was folded into building output. It now raises what workers add to the buildings they staff, which is 80% of a fully staffed building's listed rate, and it is added on top of the other production bonuses, outside the all-production cap. With Tool Making a fully staffed building makes 12% more than its listed rate; an empty one gains nothing. `rates` shows the gain as its own Worker bonus part. A first run reaches the Modern Age about 5% sooner (a median of 5.1 days for the smoke bot, from 5.3), inside the 4.8 to 6.2 day band.
- **Cyberpunk Age research is paced**: Cybernetics costs 14B knowledge (was 5.5M), so it finishes about 26 hours into the age, with Holography about 11 hours after it. All three Cyberpunk techs used to be done in the first 11 hours.
- **Fusion Age research is paced**: Plasma Physics (9.5B knowledge, was 9M), Superconductors (4.8B, was 11M, now after Plasma Physics) and Maglev Transit run one after another and finish about 17, 24 and 32 hours into the age, instead of all of Fusion's techs being done in its first 12 hours and then 26 hours of nothing new before the wonder.
- **The Information and Cyberpunk Ages run faster**: their producers repay in 0.8x the time (Information 14.8 hours, Cyberpunk 22), so they make 25% more. Both ran 1.2 to 1.5x their targets, mostly waiting. The Info Vault now holds 1.2T of each resource (was 930B) and the Cyber Vault 10.8T (was 8.6T), so their storage still holds 4.5 hours of the larger income.
- **A first run takes about a week now (it was about three days).** Every age from the Bronze Age on is 2.6 times longer: Bronze 3h 54m, Iron 6h 30m, Classical 9h 6m, Medieval 11h 42m, Renaissance 15h 36m, Colonial 18h 12m, Industrial 20h 48m, Victorian 23h 24m, Electric 26h, Atomic and Modern 31h 12m, Information 36h 24m, and up to 62h 24m from the Interstellar Age. The Primitive and Stone Ages stay at 15 and 45 minutes. The economy follows the targets, so from the Bronze Age on a producer makes 1/2.6 as much per tick, and building and research times may run longer (they are capped at a share of the age). The smoke-test bot reaches the Modern Age in about 5.3 days (was 2.2). A player who checks in every hour gets there in about 5.6 days, every 3 hours in 6.5 and every 8 hours in 9.2 (were 2.6, 3.6 and 6.2). Everything that counted real time runs 2.6 times longer from the Bronze Age on, so an age has as much going on as before, not more: the wait between random events (13 to 52 minutes, was 5 to 20) and each event's length and cooldown, epoch events and awakenings, war raids (every 3m 28s, was 80 seconds) and the quiet spell that ends a war (26 minutes), opinion drift, worker loans, new faction deals (every 2h 36m, was every hour), trade route runs, expeditions and campaigns, the Geographic Society's dispatches, faction boons and setbacks, the festival (13 minutes, then 26 minutes to the next) and the black market (20m 48s between deals), milestone chain speed boosts, and Endure's Reconstruction Effort (562 ticks, was 216). Gifts of resources from faction boons are 2.6 times smaller instead, since they are sized to a few ticks of production. Survivor asks for about 14h 26m of play (was 5h 33m) and Enduring Civilization about 3 days (was 1d 3h). - **The dashboard holds together on any terminal from 80x24 up.** Its rows used to be written for a screen 144 columns wide and left to the terminal to wrap on anything narrower, the size the wiki calls large (120x40) included: a resource's bar fell to a second line, a rate could break after its slash (`+12.7/` then `tick`), and the Resources box cut off every resource past the fourth or fifth with no way to reach the rest. Every box now writes its rows for the width it is drawn at. Each resource is one row on one grid (name, amount over cap, rate, bar, marker), with the rate column as wide as the widest rate shown, so the bars line up. A narrow box gives up the bar first, then the wide spacing, then the cap, and at 80 columns writes `/tick` as `/t` and shortens long names (`Dark Matter Crystals` is `D. M. Crystals`). A short box closes the rows up, the column gives the Resources box more rows when it needs them, and when even that is not enough the box shows a page of its rows and says so (`1-21 of 26 · Ctrl+R next page`): **Ctrl+R** turns the page. Faith and culture sit on the same grid, with their detail (the band in full and the epoch odds, what the next culture step gives) on a line under the row when there is room. Under construction fits its rows the same way and counts what it cannot show (`+3 more under way`). In the Buildings list a cost breaks between its parts, a rate and the crew it is quoted for stay whole, and the workers line drops its bar before it would overflow. The Next Age row never breaks a requirement in two, and on a narrow screen counts the ones already met (`✓ 2 met`) and then the ones it has no room for. The Panels list is set in two columns when the screen is short, so all sixteen show at 100x30 and 80x24. The status bar gives up its hint, then the wording of its badges, before it would clip, and the Workers box writes a long count short (`8.31K/51.8M`). Nothing changes on a screen wide enough for the old layout except that the bars line up.
- **Nothing trains before soldiers are unlocked, and nothing is counted.** A War Camp or a Barracks can stand before the Iron Age brings soldiers. No soldier was ever added (a locked store takes nothing), but the Army panel showed a training rate, and Stats counted that rate as soldiers gathered every tick, none of them ever held. A resource the age has not unlocked now has a rate of 0, so nothing counts it, and the Army panel says `Training: not yet. Soldiers arrive in the Iron Age.` (the age is named only once you can see it). Totals already in a save are left as they are. The soldier store the panel shows was right all along: shared storage counts toward it, as the Military page says.
- **Panels.** The age splash's upgrade counts line up however long a building's name is. A boon in the Factions panel names its civilization once (it read `Ironhold Clans: Ironhold Clans: Specialty Windfall`). The skyline map's status line said `map flows flows` and cut off the district's name at 100 columns; it now reads `map flows overlay` and names its keys as fully as the width allows. The History panel's footer names the ages you advanced into (it printed keys like `stone_age`), and a flat series carries its value once on the axis instead of three times. In the Help panel a description that wraps continues under itself, not under the command column, and a new section lists the dashboard's own keys. The Buildings list capitalizes every worker domain (`Metallurgy`, `Energy`).
- **The mini map shows from 105x34, and the game and the wiki now say so.** They said "about 120x40". The `minimap on` reply and the Map, Commands, How to Play and Your First Age pages quote the size the layout works out, and a test holds them to it.
- Wiki: on a phone the text column is as wide as the screen less one 16px gutter a side (it was 253px of 375, the theme's padding and the site's both applied). The search no longer asks for `/README.md` on every page load: the sidebar's link back to the site is marked so search skips it. The dashboard pictures are drawn at 120x40.
- Tests: the dashboard is drawn at 80x24, 100x30, 120x40 and 144x46 in the Primitive, Medieval and Quantum Ages and every box is held to what it wrote, so a wrapped or cut row, a split rate or a missing panel name fails (`TestDashboardRowsNeverWrap`); `Ctrl+R` must reach all 26 resources at every size; the fitted rows are checked in every theme. `DASHBOARD_LAYOUT_DUMP=<dir>` writes the staged dashboards as text.
- **Auto-recruit starved workers on mastered ages.** Era Mastery multiplies the net food rate, and the recruit check compared that with a worker's food before the speed-up, so on known ground the food looked two to four times richer than it was: with the same buildings a 2x age hired 19 workers where a first run hires 15, and food fell. The check now reads the food grown before the speed-up, so a mastered age recruits exactly what a first run does. The Workers panel's "feeds up to N workers" was inflated the same way and uses the same figure.
- **A Succumb cut the starting stock off at 50.** Starting food and wood were added before storage was sized, so the new run's stores were still at their base 50 and anything over that was lost; the first snapshot after a Succumb also showed base storage until the first tick. Succumb now sizes storage first, as a prestige does.
- **Festival and black market cooldowns got stuck after a Succumb.** Both are kept as tick numbers, and a Succumb sent the tick counter back to 0 without resetting them: a festival cooldown with 780 ticks left at tick 5,001 had 5,781 left in the new run. They start over with the run now, as at a prestige, and so does the Geographic Society's survey countdown. A save already stuck heals on load: a cooldown is never longer than a fresh one.
- **The Research panel listed storage, housing and flat output as percentages.** Its Research bonuses list printed every tech effect as a percent of something: `+37500% All production` for +375 storage, `+500% Housing` for +5 housing, `+280% Food production` for +2.8 food/tick. It now lists the percentage bonuses as percentages and the rest as amounts (`Output: +2.8 food/tick`, `Storage: +375 for every resource`, `Housing: +5`).
- **A tech's flat output also raised storage.** Research bonuses were kept by target alone, so Agriculture's +0.5 food/tick added 0.5 food storage and Mercantilism's +2 gold/tick added 2 gold storage. They are kept by effect type now.
- **Embassy text.** An Embassy's opinion is split evenly across the civilizations that are not hostile; its description read as if each one got the full amount (`+0.05 opinion/tick per worker with every civilization`). It now says `split across the civilizations that are not hostile`. A building of any key with an opinion effect now counts, not only the two embassies.
- **Event log lines quoted the wrong duration.** From the Bronze Age on every timed random event runs 2.6 times its base length, and its log line still quoted the base one: Gold Rush said `for ~30s` and ran 78 seconds. The line now says how long the event lasts in the age it fired in.
- **A grant a full store cut short now says so.** An event, a milestone reward or a boon's lump states its full amount (`+250 food`); storage takes only what fits. The next log line now says what you got: `Storage was nearly full: only 25 food (of 250) fit.`
- **Buildings that make a resource before it unlocks say so.** A locked resource gathers nothing, so a Uranium Mine made no uranium in the Victorian and Electric Ages while its description promised +41 a tick. Nineteen buildings are in that position (uranium, titanium ore, titanium, plasma, dark matter, quantum flux and antimatter producers, the War Camp and Barracks, the Colosseum's culture). Each now reads `once unlocked` beside that output, and the Buildings wiki page lists them.
- **The Void Rift event** granted 5K dark matter two ages before dark matter unlocks, into a store the player could not see. It grants dark matter crystals. **Mine Collapse** no longer claims a coal penalty: it fires from the Iron Age and coal unlocks in the Renaissance Age.
- **The Cultural Festival epoch event** is no longer rolled on entering the Iron Era: it pays in culture, which unlocks an age later.
- **The Research panel listed research times without research speed.** A tech showed its base time however much research speed you had, so the bonus only showed once the research was running. Each tech now lists the time it takes if started now, and the panel's header says what your research speed does. Research speed is read from one pool, the one the Stats panel lists, so a wonder or boon that grants it in the future counts without extra wiring.
- **The History panel's All production graph** showed only the milestone and epoch event part of the bonus. It now shows the bonus the game applies: every source, held to the cap.
- Military buildings no longer carry the `capacity: military` marker their soldier storage and training are derived from: the engine never read it.
- **`sell` suggested buildings it then refused.** Its completions listed every building you had, wonders and storage included, though neither can be sold, and offered buildings in the Primitive Age, where nothing can be sold. It now suggests only what `sell` takes, and a test holds the two together.
- **Boon crews were invisible.** The temporary workers a faction boon lends (*Extra Hands*) showed nowhere, so there was no way to tell who sent them or when they would leave. The Factions panel now lists each crew under Boons and setbacks: who sent it, how many workers and the time left. A crew from a civilization you have not met doesn't name it.
- **A `plan advance` while you were away no longer leaves the next age's buildings unstaffed.** Nothing recruited while you were away, so once the idle workers and the workers the plan could move out of superseded buildings ran out, the next age's producers sat at the unstaffed 20% until you came back. The shares routine now recruits into them as housing and food allow.
- **A catastrophe could leave you stuck in an age for good.** Endure and the Great Fire destroyed storage like any other building, but the age lock never lets an older age's storage be rebuilt, and an age's first storage copy can cost more than the storage left after such a loss. A smoke run entered the Victorian Age with 478M of storage, lost both Industrial Depots and 11 older storage copies to a Nuclear Exchange, and was left with 130M: the first Victorian Vault (about 210M steel) never fit, so no cap could rise again and nothing the age required could ever be bought. Storage is now permanent, like wonders: Endure, the Great Fire and Succumb's ruins never take it (Endure's 20% is counted from the other buildings), and it can't be sold.
- **Smoke missed storage that could never grow.** The storage invariant counted every storage copy left in the age as reachable, even when the first one couldn't fit, so the stall above surfaced only as a 36-hour age timeout. Storage is now counted a copy at a time, a stall is reported as `storage_unreachable` and leads the blocked-on line, and the static check gains a storage ladder clause: each age's first storage copy must fit 1.25x under the least storage its gate forces a player to hold.
- **The main window shows your commands' results again.** A gather, a build started or finished, a sale, workers assigned or a research started had moved to the Logs panel only. They are back in the main window's log, which now leaves out the tick number (`T133`); the Logs panel keeps it on every line.
- **"3 Barrackses."** Building names that end in s got an extra "es" in logs and milestone rows ("Barrackses", "Coal Workses"). Names that are already plural stay as they are (Barracks, Ironworks, the Works and Steelworks, Standing Stones), Fortress, Campus and Nexus take "es", and the Monument of Ages becomes "Monuments of Ages".
- **The Milestones panel never listed the epoch milestones.** Its Progress total counted all six (First Farmers, Survivor, Enduring Civilization, Age Hopper, Industrial Titan, Power Grid), but the panel kept its own list of groups and Epoch wasn't on it. They now have their own Epoch group between Faith and Ages, under the same no-spoiler rule as the rest: Age Hopper stays hidden until the Classical Age is your next age.
- **Temporary workers from a faction boon never left.** The Extra Hands boon lends its crew for a set time (the log says how long), but nothing sent them home, so they stayed for the rest of the run. They now leave when their time is up (counted while you play, like the timed boons), idle workers first; a crew that was staffing buildings takes those workers with it, and the log says how many. The loan is saved with your game.
- **Prestige kept the old run's timers.** A run that ended ready to advance let `advance` skip the Stone Age's requirements until the first tick of the new run, a run that ended in a famine logged "Food is back" into the new one, and the Geographic Society's survey countdown carried over. Prestige and a new game now start all three over, as a fresh game does.
- **`account wipe` said a wipe keeps your saves.** It deletes them: wiping an account removes its whole slot, every save included, after backing the slot up to `data/backups/`. The `account wipe` and `account` replies, the Commands page and the Account page now say so. The wipe's confirm dialogs already did.
- **Game speed carried across prestige.** Prestige reset everything but the speed setting, so a run that prestiged at 7x started the next run at 7x. A prestige now goes back to 1x, as Succumb and a wipe already did, and a save that carries a higher speed loads at 1x, offline catch-up included.
- **`account recover` wiped the account in use.** It wrote an empty account under the recovered ID over whichever account was active, losing its achievements, stats and themes, and recovering your own code reset you. Each account now saves only to its own slot: recovery restores the code's account into its own slot (opening it as it is if it is already on this machine, never overwriting it) and then switches to it. The confirm step now covers any progress the account in use holds (theme unlocks, achievements or lifetime stats), says what it holds, and recovering your own code does nothing.
- **Switching or importing accounts during a game crossed accounts.** `account switch` left the game running under the new account: records since the last autosave were lost, autosave wrote the run into the new account's saves, and later prestiges counted for it. A game now belongs to the account it was started or loaded under. Switching (or recovering) during a game saves the game to that account, stops it and returns to the main menu. Import never switches accounts any more and never points the active account at another slot, a backup of the account in use goes straight into it, and a late save (the autosave, the exit handler) always lands in the file of the account that owns the data.
- **A hand-edited account.json stopped showing as modified after the next save.** The flag was never written, so any save re-signed the edit. It is now saved (signed, and only when set) and sticks through saves, hand edits and export/import. Untampered files keep their bytes and signatures.
- **The developer console earned account records.** With dev mode on, `/age modern_age` then prestige granted First Prestige, and `/age galactic_age` unlocked all five flavor themes for good. A run any dev command has changed (god mode included) now records nothing to the account, logs one line saying so, and keeps the mark in its save through prestige until a new game. Dev mode on without dev commands still records as usual.
- **The catastrophe modal showed the unbraced losses after you bought Brace.** Its Endure section now shows what Endure will actually cost, with Brace and your garrison applied, and says what each took off. The reconstruction debuff reads in minutes, not ticks.
- **War raids reported losses that never happened.** A raid that finds less than it would carry off takes nothing, and the log now says so instead of "lost 150 dark_matter". Raid lines use resource names, not keys.
- **Events never said how much they actually took.** Events that steal resources or drive off workers now log what left your stores when they strike ("You lost 10 food and 5 gold."), under the event line that gives the most it can take. Timed events used to report workers lost only when they ended.
- **Expedition and campaign results gave no amounts.** "Gained loot." is now "Loot: 60 food, 60 wood, 20 stone.", and a failure says what 30% came back. No exclamation marks on routine results.
- **Campaign and expedition wording.** Military missions are campaigns and scouting missions are expeditions everywhere in the refusals and launch lines; age refusals name the age ("needs the Iron Age") instead of printing a key; every refusal says what to do next. The Army panel formats costs with resource names, drops "Soldiers: 0" from scouting cards, explains difficulty, and says campaigns need soldiers from the Iron Age.
- **Endure and Succumb log lines.** Endure reports morale, reads its percentages from the numbers it applies, and says "You endured the Iron Era" instead of "Survived marker earned". Succumb's legacy line lists the bonus it grants.
- **`wonder bank food all` did nothing.** The command only knew `wonder collect`, so `wonder bank …` quietly showed the bank status and deposited nothing. `bank` now works as `collect`; the amount can be a number, `all` or `max` (as much as the wonder still needs, up to what you have), or left off (the same as `all`); and `wonder bank all` banks every resource the wonder needs. Each deposit reports what actually went in (it used to echo the amount typed even when the bank took less), and a refusal says why: the wonder is built, it doesn't need that resource, that part is full, you have none, or you have less than the amount. Autocomplete offers the resources the wonder still needs and the amount words.
- **Same seed, same run on every machine.** A run played on Apple Silicon (or any arm64) drifted from the same run on an x86 PC within a few hundred ticks, so a save could play on differently after moving between them and local smoke numbers didn't match CI. The arm64 compiler fused multiply-adds that x86 rounds in two steps, and `math.Pow`, `Exp` and `Log` return different last bits per architecture. Simulation code now rounds every such product and uses its own portable `Pow`, `Log10` and friends (package `detmath`); x86 runs play out exactly as before. A new CI workflow plays the same seeds on Linux x86, Linux arm64 and macOS arm64 and fails if they differ.
- **Completion drift.** The completion list was kept by hand apart from the command handler and kept falling behind it (it lacked `diplomacy tribute`, `raid` and `acct`, and offered `research list list`). It is now the command registry, held to the handler by a test.
- **Help** said `gather` takes at most 10; it takes 25. `quit` is now listed in Help and the Commands page.
- **Iron Age gold trap.** Reaching the Iron Age without a Bronze Age market left no way to get gold: the trading post, the only gold producer and the only trade building (the market needs one), cost gold, and so did agoras, legion forts, temples and the Classical Age's requirement. The trading post is now priced in stone and iron only. The automated age-gate check (the "Gate Covenant") now asks whether every resource a gate needs can be had by a player who skipped every building no earlier gate required, so a trap of this kind fails the build; it found no others.
- **Commands needed Enter twice.** While the suggestion list was open, the first Enter only took the suggestion (`advance` became `advance `), so `advance`, `status`, `research list` and the rest ran on the second press. Enter now runs what you typed on the first press; Tab still takes the highlighted suggestion.
- **`build` queues several storage copies.** Building a storage building a second time while the first copy was under construction was refused ("already under construction"), though `build <storage> 5` queued five. Now each `build` queues another copy, up to the building's cap. Only unique buildings still refuse a second copy.
- **Keys were case-sensitive in some commands.** `build Hut` worked but `sell Hut` and `dismiss Hut` said the building was unknown, and so did mixed-case keys in `research`, `expedition`, `campaign`, `diplomacy`, `trade route start|stop` and `prestige buy`. Every command now reads its building, tech, resource, civilization, theme, expedition, route and upgrade keys in any case.
- **The Commands page's shortcut table was wrong.** It listed `e`, `m`, `w` and `l`, which are not commands, and gave `r`, `s` and `t` as panels when they are `recruit`, `status` and `trade`. It now lists the real short names, and the docs check holds it to the command registry. How to Play and the Workers page no longer point at those keys either.
- **The plan left the new age's producers unstaffed after `plan advance`.** When the plan advanced while you were away, the next age's buildings it then finished sat at the unstaffed 20% until your next visit: the old age's copies had already taken every idle worker, and the plan only staffed from idle ones. Now, when idle workers run out, the plan moves workers in from buildings the advance superseded (a higher tier of their line is open), the same line's first. It never moves food workers or this age's workers, and the plan itself never recruits (the worker shares routine does; see the fix above).
- **The Trade panel had an out-of-date Diplomacy section.** It kept its own list of civilizations and still said to "reach Colonial Age to discover factions", though first contact comes from expeditions. The section is gone; faction standing lives on the Factions panel, and the Trade panel's last line points to it (`factions`). The panel now shows only the faction effect that changes trade income: an **Allied Bonuses** block listing each ally's bonus to its specialty resource.
- **Grand Library Built could never complete.** It asked for 5 Great Libraries, and the Great Library is a wonder you can build once. It now asks for the Great Library.
- **Tech Ascendant said 52 techs.** There are 73, and the milestone ("every technology, researched") now asks for all 73. Its count follows the tech tree, so it stays right when techs are added.
- **Every milestone and chain can be completed.** Seven milestones never could: the last Stone Pit, Temple, Trading Post or Coal Plant they asked for cost more than its age can store, a billion and ten billion people need more housing than the game has, and 20,000 structures is more than a run holds. Three more were out of reach before prestige (10 million and 100 million people, 5,000 structures), so the Settlement, Builder and Trade chains could never finish. Retuned, with the rewards unchanged: Stone Mason 30 Stone Pits (was 50), Temple City 25 Temples (50), Power Grid 20 Coal Plants and 10 Steam Turbines (50 and 10), and Trade Empire 20 Trading Posts and Merchant Quarters in any mix (30 posts and 12 quarters), so upgrading posts into quarters keeps the progress. The population ladder is 250, 1,500, 5,000, 15,000, 50,000, 250,000 and 1,000,000 (was 5,000 up to 10 billion), and Global City needs the Digital Age. The structures ladder is 250, 500, 1,000 and 2,000 (was 500, 2,000, 5,000 and 20,000). Tech Master now names the Information Age, Wonder Empire the Digital Age and Tech Ascendant the Transcendent Age: the ages where 50 techs, 15 wonders and every tech first become possible. See the Milestones wiki page.
- **Four trade routes opened before you could build what they need.** Cotton Exchange and Steamship Line wanted a Seaport (Modern Age) and Rail Freight wanted Steam Works (Victorian Age), all from the Industrial Age; Warp Commerce opened in the Space Age and wanted a Warp Drive Plant (Interstellar Age). Cotton Exchange now needs 1 Stock Exchange, Steamship Line 2 Harbor Authorities and Rail Freight 1 Integrated Steelworks, all Industrial Age buildings; Warp Commerce now opens in the Interstellar Age.

### Removed
- **The design documents.** They are no longer kept in the repository.
- **The `speed` command and the wonder speed cap.** Game speed is fixed at 1x so the calendar paces the game. Each wonder raised the speed cap by 0.5x and every age advance needs its age's wonder, so a player who kept `speed` at the cap ran at 7x by the Atomic Age and reached the Modern Age in about 13 hours instead of about 54, which undercut every pacing target. The command is gone from Help, completion and the Commands page, and typing it gets the unknown-command reply; wonders no longer mention a speed cap, and the status bar and Stats panel no longer show the speed setting. Game speed bonuses from techs, prestige, events and milestone chains still apply, and the dev console keeps `/speed`.
- **The old City Map and World Map** (`ui/citymap`, about 29.7k lines with its tests), replaced by the Map panel, along with the unused `assets/` embed package and the `map_demos/` PNGs. `citymap` and `worldmap` remain as aliases of `map`.
- **`catastrophe invoke`.** Deliberately facing a catastrophe (to Succumb for a legacy bonus) moves to the Harbinger's Invite. The command only ever worked in the Stone Era anyway, where it made a free invoke-and-Succumb loop. Developers can still force one with the dev console's `/catastrophe`.

### Fixed
- A wonder bank could never fill when its rounded price came out as 999999.9999999999 (the Parthenon's stone): the cost rounding now multiplies by an exact power of ten, and a bank within 0.001 of its price counts as full, as the deposit command already assumed.
- Building descriptions now always show the building's real production rate.
- The `prestige` status said to reach the Medieval Age to prestige; it now says the Modern Age.
- Theme picker no longer deadlocks the app on arrow/Esc.
- Danger dialogs (wipe data, wipe account, delete save) were unreadable on some themes — the account-wipe panel drew red text on its red fill. They now read on every theme.

- Save-name modal: readable input contrast, opaque background, sized to content.
- Load Game: bare `load` opens the browser; renaming re-parents child saves; footer hotkeys render as keycaps.
- Modifiers: negative production debuffs apply correctly and build-cost modifiers are wired in.
- Stash is buildable again (its first-copy cost had exceeded the base wood cap).
- Tick loop and UI snapshot no longer rebuild the building table (and other config tables) every tick; a late-game tick is ~13x faster.
- Age-advance splash no longer freezes the game when an epoch catastrophe rolls on the same advance; the catastrophe modal now opens after the splash is dismissed.
- Catastrophe and Ancient Memory modal buttons show their labels (they rendered blank) and their keyboard shortcuts.
- Catastrophe Endure counted wonders when working out the 20% of buildings to destroy (while never destroying them); it now counts only destroyable buildings.
- Catastrophe Endure left workers assigned to buildings it destroyed; they now return to the idle pool before the 25% worker loss.
- A random catastrophe roll could overwrite one that was still pending, and the new one's modal never appeared. That can no longer happen.
- The catastrophe toast never fired (no event was published); it now shows when a catastrophe strikes.
- The catastrophe modal covered the whole dashboard with blank space and had see-through rows; it is now a box sized to its content, floating over the visible dashboard.
- The Epoch tab showed a pending or overwritten catastrophe as "Survived"; it now shows Survived, Succumbed, Pending, or "outcome not recorded" for old saves.
- The Stats panel counted succumbs as survived; Survived now means Endured, and Succumbed counts every succumb.
- `recruit 9223372036854775807` overflowed the population check and left the population near -9.2 quintillion. Counts are now whole numbers from 1 to 1,000,000 and amounts must be positive numbers; anything else is refused with the usage line (`trade food wood NaN` and `wonder collect food NaN` used to turn a resource into NaN).
- Loading a game dropped building upgrades offered in an earlier age (a lineage with no tier in the current age lost its offer, and `upgrade gathering_camp` said there was nothing to upgrade). Saves now keep the offers.
- Loading an Iron Age save unlocked coal, which normal play unlocks in the Renaissance Age.
- Loading a game restarted its random events, expeditions and encounters from the run's first roll. A loaded game now carries on the same random stream it was saved with.
- Research bonuses could come back a hair off after a load (0.7999999999999999 instead of 0.8).
- After a prestige, the storage upgrade didn't apply until the first tick, and starting resources beyond the base storage were cut off (Starting Food tier 3 gave 35 food, not 75).
- `dump` wrote its file to a `data/logs` folder relative to wherever the game was launched; it now goes in `logs/` in your account's data folder.
- Upgrading only some copies of a building left all its workers on the copies that remained, more than they have slots for. Workers over the remaining slots now move to the upgraded building, or go idle if it's full.
- Loading a game reset the festival and black-market cooldowns, and a prestige left them counting against the old run's ticks. Both are saved now and reset on prestige.
- Selling a storage building left stock above the new, lower cap for anything not being produced.
- Autocomplete offers `diplomacy tribute`, `diplomacy raid`, `account wipe` and the `acct` alias, and no longer re-offers an argument you've already typed (`research list list`).
