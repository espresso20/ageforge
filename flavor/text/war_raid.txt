# How this file is written (the same in every file of this folder):
#
#   A line that starts with # is a comment. Blank lines are skipped.
#
#   "== words NAME" starts a noun bank. Each line under it is one entry, written
#   as it lands in the middle of a sentence: lowercase, no full stop.
#
#   "== NAME" starts a pool of sentences. NAME is the pool's name. Add eras=
#   (ancient, feudal, industrial, digital, cosmic) or ages= (age keys) to hold
#   the pool to those eras or ages, with commas between them. With neither, the
#   pool fits every age.
#
#   Under a pool, one line is one sentence, written as "tags | text".
#     text  has no full stop (the game adds it) and at most one ~, where an
#           entry of the noun bank named by slot= goes. The game fills in
#           {subject}, {res}, {amt_res} and the other {placeholders}.
#     tags  a register (plain, wry or joke), a form if it has one (ledger,
#           complaint, overheard or notice) and a topic, then any of tone=,
#           kind=, need= and slot=, with commas between several values.
#
#   Lines are numbered by their place in the pool or bank, and the numbers are
#   the row ids of the copy sheets and of the VibeMode script. Add new lines at
#   the end of a pool: a line put in the middle renumbers every line after it.

# WarRaid — a civilization you are at war with came over the wall and took
# something.
#
# The log line directly above this one already names the raider, names the
# resource and gives the number. So nothing here says what was taken or how
# much. These sentences are the morning after: what burnt and what stood next
# to it, who saw them coming and who slept through it, the state of the outer
# wall, what they left in the mud, the counting, somebody's child who is
# missing and somebody's child who is not, and the rebuilding that started
# before the ash went cold. See catalog.go for the rules.
#
# This is the grimmest Moment in the catalog and it carries the smallest
# humour budget of the five — under one line in twelve reaches for anything,
# and the flat lines are what make those land. Grim is not the same as
# melodramatic. The strongest sentences here are the plainest: what a person
# did with their hands the next morning.

# both are drawn by ungated sentences and so have to be true of a stone camp
# and of a ring station alike. That rules out more than the lint catches —
# nothing here is a cart, a hearth or a window shutter.
